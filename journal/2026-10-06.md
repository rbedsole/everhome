## A permanent home

After comparing candidate landscapes, the chosen site put the important pieces together: broad plains for future development and a mountain immediately beside them for the permanent storage complex. From this point forward, the site is no longer "C1." It is **Everhome**.

The former starter base at **-890, 65, -474** is abandoned. Its Nether portal and early mining infrastructure remain part of the world's history, but they do not define the permanent settlement or its future transport network.

## Everhome begins

Everhome's main residence was placed with WorldEdit at the foot of the mountain. The complete initial build occupies a **41 × 41** footprint, and after correcting its orientation the main entrance faces **west**.

The build itself did not damage the terrain. To create enough level ground for the footprint, however, part of the site was filled with grass. One resulting corner now projects unnaturally from the original terrain and is the first small cleanup job for the next session.

Everhome is intended to contain the everyday functions of the base: living space, crafting, equipment work, and convenient working storage. The surrounding settlement will grow outward through farms and production buildings rather than duplicating those core functions.

## Storage has a destination

The mountain storage build has already been chosen. It will be excavated into the mountain with WorldEdit and constructed as a dedicated major project. The finished system will use **automatic sorting**.

Recent planning also identified several mods that can support the system without dictating its visible design: Nether Chests for dense bulk storage, Hopper Gadgetry for routing and filtering, Linked Chests for useful remote access, and the Gold Bag of Holding for mobile cargo.

The guiding idea is simple: **visible storage is architecture; hidden storage is infrastructure.**

## A settlement, not just a base

A village already exists near Everhome. Rather than dismantling it, the current idea is to preserve it as a neighbor and eventually bring two villagers to Everhome. Villagers can be bred locally, allowing a new population to grow alongside the settlement.

Trading Post and Trade Cycling make it practical to let villagers occupy useful buildings instead of requiring a conventional trading hall.

FarmTweaks and Toss To Feed similarly support the idea that Everhome's farms can look like farms while still gaining useful automation. Crop fields, barns, lumber operations, and later industrial resource production can become real parts of the settlement as they are needed.

## The road ahead

The current roadmap begins with settling into Everhome and establishing its own Nether access. The mountain storage complex follows as the first major construction project. Renewable production, a local villager population, a permanent quarry operation, and a new Nether transport network can then grow from actual needs rather than from a rigid master checklist.

Simple Quarries will eventually receive a dedicated mining facility, but the building will serve as the permanent operational and processing center rather than being tied forever to a single shaft. Quarry heads can move to fresh extraction areas as older sites reach the bottom of the world.

Everhome has stopped being a destination being searched for. It is now the place the rest of the world grows from.
